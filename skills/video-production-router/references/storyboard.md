# Storyboard before new multi-shot production

Use this original Framewright adapter when creating a new film from a brief and
no useful shot plan exists. A short launch can use a compact shot table; a complex
film may need per-shot prompts. Do not impose a full storyboard on comparison,
finished-media QA, a simple recut, supplied approved shots, or a single-shot prompt.
For a prompt-only task with absent artwork, keep the asset prerequisite explicit
inside the concise prompt: use the approved logo once supplied; do not fabricate
missing artwork. This preserves uncertainty without intake questions, extra shots
or implying that a file or its rights were inspected.
An explicit renderer remains fixed; storyboard is preproduction, not its replacement.

The upstream [AI Video Storyboard skill](https://github.com/aicontentskills/ai-video-storyboard-skill/blob/93f8a6d6935858bc4acd7ff3bbea2411edf88caa/SKILL.md)
contributes the shared-visual-language / coordinated-shot-prompt pattern. This is
an original integration contract, not a vendored copy. Upstream README/SKILL state
MIT; that revision has no standalone LICENSE and lacks its four referenced guides.
Use only available material. Do not invent successful reads of missing references.

## Lock the brief before assigning tools

Read product facts. Extract intended viewers, language, CTA, duration, canvas/fps,
available authorised assets, explicit tools, style, hard prohibitions and budget.
Ask only about unresolved choices that change the plan. Mark assumptions as such.
A proposed commercial budget is not verified pricing, credit availability, rights
or consent to purchase. A storyboard cannot clear assets or generated music rights.

Set a continuity sheet: palette/type hierarchy, light/texture, camera/motion rules,
recurring asset IDs, typography/caption language and audio role. Style is a target;
no plan establishes visual consistency in outputs before they exist. Respect silent,
reduced-motion, 4K, square and other explicit requirements over upstream defaults.

## Make an executable handoff, not a promise of generation

Use a shot table with **ID; start/end or frame range; purpose; source asset IDs;
copy; action/camera; picture tool; audio/captions; evidence gaps; QA**. Assign tools
by actual inputs and requested functions, not genre alone. Examples: real React
panels → Flute; authored typography → Remotion; existing artwork → an appropriate
montage renderer; object illustrations → Anatomy only within prototype scope.
One shared timeline and continuity sheet span all selected tools.

Write a concrete per-shot instruction where useful. For authored scenes describe
layers, keyframes and intended transitions; for a selected AI generator describe
the subject/action/environment plus permitted style and technical target. A prompt
does not prove the tool supports that feature. Do not add generic "synchronized
audio" to silent work, force 1080p, fabricate product UI, or invent social proof to
fill a storytelling slot. Source facts and approved artwork remain authoritative.

Keep exact timing: sum shot durations **minus transition overlaps** must match the
master duration. At fixed fps use end-exclusive frame ranges. Music-first retains
approved track/cue points; video-first retains picture timing. Measure real speech
before finalising cuts; absent narration leaves duration validation blocked.
Do not force five-second shots or a fixed shot count onto every brief.

## Output and boundaries

Deliver the storyboard/continuity sheet plus source-to-copy comparison, asset/rights
status, intended per-tool inputs/outputs and the [QA handoff](handoff.md). Record
whether the plan is proposed or already approved; do not invent owner acceptance.
An approved storyboard does not authorise new spending, accounts or publication.
Existing explicit authorisation remains valid within its scope.

The agent then follows the chosen renderer's installed guide when execution is
requested and authorised. Storyboarding itself does not install, generate clips,
render, download, buy credits or publish. A text plan has no MP4, decoded-media QA,
measured render cost or audience result. A successful shot/tool does not qualify
the whole composite film. Skip unneeded layers rather than extending the task.

For a short story-led book/project promotion, use the optional
[narrative-promo module](narrative-promos.md). It adds a causal/action and cue
handoff to this same contract; it is not a new renderer or execution grant.
