# Two-host micro-lessons — original studio recipe

Use for an authorised short educational film, usually90–150seconds. The first
[Framewright lesson](../../../docs/experiments/framewright-micro-lesson.md) adapts
Claudemy's dialogue/source-card/one-action pattern to our existing production chain.
Native Claudemy plugin, scraper and ElevenLabs dialogue were not executed. This is
an original adapter, not a replacement learning platform or a blanket tool qualification.

1. **One concrete outcome.** Define what the viewer will understand and one small
   action they can try. Start with a pilot, not an automatically generated12-part
   course. Use primary product docs for first-party explanations; broader topics
   need their own source/research plan. Record exact source revisions and claim
   support, not just a URL inventory. Page counts are not expert consensus.
2. **Write the dialogue and pictures together.** Questioner asks useful questions;
   explainer answers with a real example and a practical consequence. Avoid filler
   agreement. Each turn has id, role/voice, text, section and source IDs. Each shot
   names the visible action and real material. Clearly distinguish diagrams from
   the actual product interface. A game or a video example needs a real retained
   clip, not a caption claiming it exists. Remove source audio unless cleared and
   deliberately needed. Keep original media unchanged.
3. **Measure the voices.** Use an explicitly selected approved engine/budget. Never
   switch to paid speech merely because an API key exists. Local SupertonicF1/M1
   produced this English trial; raw takes, voice/model hashes and measured per-turn
   boundaries are retained. Keep turns/stems separate, use small pauses and a quiet
   cleared/original score. Measure the final mix/encode, not only source WAV peaks.
   Two synthetic styles are not a cloned person. Word-count duration is an estimate.
4. **Review targeted uncertainties.** ASR screens script mismatches; it does not
   certify pronunciation/listening. In this trial a repeated ambiguity at “Brighten”
   motivated a clearer replacement, while a missing closing phrase in whole-file
   ASR was recovered by a separate short check. Preserve those findings. Align
   captions to ASR word anchors constrained by actual turn intervals; keep script
   spelling for product names. Inspect subtitle timing/crop separately from video.
5. **Deliver a real film and a local kit.** Render one common timeline with actual
   clips, labels/diagrams and speech activity derived from PCM. Check full decode,
   source/role/cut alignment, readable text and attribution, repeated seeking and
   encoded picture/audio. Inspect the text-bearing thumbnail at full/reduced sizes.
   Prepare loose video, thumbnail, captions, title, description, tags and source
   card; no archive by default. Record technical, owner listening/creative and
   publication outcomes independently. Expand a series only after pilot feedback.

The120-second English pilot is a bounded production example. Multilingual speech,
learning effectiveness, retention/revenue, automated fact checking and autonomous
publication remain unqualified. ClaudemyMIT inspiration:
https://github.com/assafkip/claudemy/tree/438a6288fa74a2edad2cc86416d28308cb02ee1a

## Optional opening personalities

When requested, introduce two fictional photo presenters briefly so dialogue roles
are visually grounded. Keep the main action readable; label synthetic presenters
and the active speaker. The first trial used two inspected6-pose atlases and original
Canvas compositing with speech-envelope mouth states, blinks and breathing. Check
actual dimensions/grid/identity consistency before using an atlas; generators do
not guarantee these. Keep the generated originals and provenance, not just crops.

This approach does not analyse phonemes or guarantee exact lip sync. Inspect actual
encoded mouth/eye transitions for flicker, ghosting and unintended identity drift;
retain owner full-motion feedback. Preserve accepted audio by copying its encoded
track where compatible, verify packet digest AND timing, and compare unaffected
body frames. A short intro is not authority to replace the hosts throughout a film
or clone a real person's likeness/voice. The owner accepted Framewright v2 for publication, while explicitly finding the
facial expressions jerky/artificial despite adequate mouth timing. Keep that defect.
Before the next similar production, review an8–12s sample with smoother mouth
transitions and independent eye/brow/head motion. Do not claim a broadly qualified
natural talking-avatar engine from this accepted one-off.
