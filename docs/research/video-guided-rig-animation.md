## Pipeline and confirmation boundaries

1. **Mesh and rig.** Tripo's [Auto Rig documentation](https://developers.tripo3d.com/en/docs/animations-rig) lists aquatic and other skeleton types, rig checking and rigged GLB/FBX output. Inspect the exact model's jaw, spine, fins, skin weights and scale before animation; documented topology support does not prove this shark's rig quality. Older upload/help text describes narrower support, so pin the endpoint/version and run its compatibility check before committing credits. Studio/API prices and entitlements are separate; a new quote is required for a trial.
2. **Procedural baseline.** Drive known bones/controls with reviewed Python, keyframes and constraints in Blender. Looping idle/swim and a jaw-open action can serve as a baseline without generative video. A Blender MCP is a command bridge, not itself a motion generator. The [community implementation](https://github.com/ahujasid/mcp-for-blender) and [Blender Lab MCP](https://www.blender.org/lab/mcp-server/) are different projects; the demo does not identify which was used. Choose the implementation/revision, inspect its execution/telemetry behaviour, use a copied scene and deliberate local access before any setup. Direct reviewed Blender scripts remain an alternative, but are not labelled a completed MCP test.
3. **Video reference.** Google's [Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash announcement](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/build-with-gemini-omni-1-1-flash/) documents generative video and frame control. It is a plausible interpretation of the supplied name, not confirmed identity of the demo's model. Use a fixed known camera, scale and distinctive markers when appropriate; retain prompt, reference frames, output and settings. Green is for separation; neither green nor markers guarantee anatomical consistency, fixed camera or stable point identity in generated frames.
4. **Return to editable 3D.** Treat the generated clip as an acting reference. Annotate visible jaw/head/tail/fin landmarks, separate root/camera movement, then fit constrained rig controls or author approximate key poses. Export/bake the resulting action and re-render it. Blender's [Object Solver](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.4/animation/constraints/motion_tracking/object_solver.html) solves object transforms, not automatically every deforming bone; [IK](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.0/animation/armatures/posing/bone_constraints/inverse_kinematics/introduction.html) supplies constrained bone-chain posing, not a complete video-to-rig system. Tripo's [retarget endpoint](https://developers.tripo3d.com/en/docs/animations-retarget) applies animation presets; it is not evidence that arbitrary MP4 motion is recovered. The fitting/authoring step is the unproven integration.
5. **Film and reuse.** Preserve one model and editable actions, then change camera, light and background for multiple shots. Publishable results still need source/model/clip rights, encoded picture/audio QA and owner review. A projected match from one camera does not uniquely recover hidden geometry or joint motion.

## Proposed staged trial

**A — local baseline and positive control.** Select an original or licensed creature/rig; no cloud generation first. Create a 4–6s procedural loop and jaw-open/close action. Save `.blend`, action/control map, camera, fps/units and deterministic renders. Before fitting generated video, test the analysis on a known marker-bearing render whose original animation is withheld from the fitting step. Compare recovered motion to that known control. This isolates whether our motion-analysis path works at all.

**B — bounded video-guided action.** Once a selected rig, provider, uploads and capped budget are approved, generate at most one short bite/gesture reference with a fixed camera. Compare it against the procedural baseline. Retain missing/occluded/reidentified landmarks explicitly; score only visible observations. Set visible-point error/coverage tolerances before fitting, report the observed values, and do not hide unmatched intervals. Bake an editable action; no claim of exact motion capture from an underdetermined view.

**C — reusable film proof.** Re-render the action under the reference camera and a second camera with new lighting. Inspect silhouette/jaw timing, bone limits, penetrations, skin collapse, temporal jitter, identity and loop seams; verify repeatable seeks and action export/reload. Include a two-shot continuity check and final decode. New-view plausibility, visible-view agreement and owner creative acceptance are separate. This could become an asset/action source for Good Dog and later mini-series, without committing to this exact shark or model family.
