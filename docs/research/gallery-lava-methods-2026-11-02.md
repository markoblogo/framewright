## Three controlled probes

Same three photo donors,14s each,960×540/24fps, identical locally synthesized score. Sequential render, external storage. A: art-directed persistent texture-bearing islands plus real wax movement. B: growing/splitting Gray-Scott patterns composited through photograph pixels. C: both. Parameters and a fixed seed remain in one private editable recipe.

Unlike the v1 handoff mask, the foreground field runs continuously across the background photograph change. Earlier-source pixels remain within the foreground until a separately timed late recolouring. The islands are an analytic artistic construction, not a physical wax or Navier-Stokes simulation. Numerical Gray-Scott evolution is separate from that construction.

Review: can viewers follow an old-image fragment into a new scene? Do separation/fusion and motion in both axes read as intentional? Does slower rhythm retain attention? Does C improve the piece or obscure its images? Does the audio support motion? Encode/decode alone does not answer these questions. All creative outcomes remain pending owner review.

## Transfer into Framewright

Retain the useful contract: source-pinned texture; persistent carrier identity/time; independent background handoff; mask/contour controls; common sound; deterministic seed; bounded output duration; provenance and public-use gates. Save selected settings and a short failure note after review. Do not promote all fluid/image-motion tools from one trial.

Possible later experiment: temporal feedback/slit-scan memory, where trails contain earlier picture states. Defer until this comparison is decided; no fourth effect added automatically. Ultra-Water and PixelFX remain separate queue entries: water surfaces/pixel effects do not directly solve this brief's viscous photographic memory.
