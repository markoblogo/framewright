# DesignBookmark Motion: Page Flip — one scoped local trial

13 November 2026. The owner identified the proposed courier story as overlapping the previously published [The Last Delivery](last-delivery.md) and requested its existing materials for a TikTok-only comparison. A 33-second vertical baseline retains its original sound; a second local candidate uses an independently authored unfolding note overlay. The owner accepted the visual comparison, chose the baseline for publication and supplied a TikTok link; see the final decision below. The 720×406, 12fps source is enlarged to 720×1280 after narrow portrait cropping, not recreated at native portrait resolution.

The tested [Page Flip](https://kokonutui.com/docs/cards/page-flip) component is by Kokonut UI and was linked from [DesignBookmark Motion](https://designbookmark.com/motion). Its upstream file declares MIT; the licence and source/lockfile are retained in the private trial. The component was installed in an isolated local React/Vite environment and, via a manual browser click, advanced from page one to the original film's note. Narrowing the frame to single-page layout resolved initial wrapping in the visual check. The React component itself was **not rendered into either video**: the candidate uses a separate Pillow/FFmpeg recreation of a simple opening card. Neither compared film used a deterministic component capture. Offline reproduction, reduced-motion verification, effort saving and product-wide qualification are not claimed. The two vertical videos are technically decodable and share the original AAC audio stream; the owner visually assessed both; independent listening and audience performance remain separate.

Private evidence: studio `productions/last-delivery-tiktok-motion/TRIAL.md` and retained source, versions and renders. Original film music is benpm CC BY 4.0 (attribution required); Quaternius robot pack is CC0. Film reuse and platform rights remain distinct from the component's MIT code licence.

Owner subsequently reported that both vertical comparison versions look acceptable. At that point this was a visual judgment of two film variants; selection and the export follow-up occurred later. Reduced-motion behavior and labor comparison remain open.

## Frame-controlled video export follow-up

At the owner's request, a local adapter added a `videoProgress` input to a private copy of the MIT component. The original fold geometry drives a Remotion composition by frame; 4 seconds were rendered to 1080×1920 at 24fps over a static frame from the old film, with no intentional sound. The renderer initially failed to start Chrome in the restricted environment and succeeded through an elevated local run without disabling the browser sandbox. The encoded clip decoded; independently rendered copies of frame 34 had the same PNG hash. This is a scoped test of an **adapted** component, not the component's own built-in export feature or an unmodified one-click workflow. The standalone insert has not been incorporated into the 33-second film or creatively accepted; reduced-motion and full-film sound integration remain open. Private evidence: `productions/last-delivery-tiktok-motion/TRIAL.md`.

Owner review: the page-turn itself looks good, but the large page obscures the scene and is not a good fit for The Last Delivery. Suggested future applications are book promotion and scientific-document reviews. This comment did not accept the insert for this film; the owner subsequently selected the baseline master.

## Closeout and retention — 13 November

The owner selected the original-scene portrait crop (variant A) and reported publication at https://vt.tiktok.com/ZSpQn9kVD/. The page-turn was visually liked but rejected for this particular film because it covered the action. This suggests a scoped future test for book promotion or a research-document review, not a claim of general suitability. The adapted component, upstream source and MIT notice, lockfile, installed dependencies, baseline, comparison and accepted direct-export evidence remain in the private trial. Its disposable npm cache was removed after checking the retained lockfile and installed dependencies. Reduced-motion, timing/cost comparison and integration into a finished film remain untested.
