## Actual bounded test

A 12.000s silent 1280×720 H.264/24fps fixture (SHA-256 `3bb01391c00b30acf430d2507e9ba0fd24fa242544740728611376bc618f1896`), three authored 4s sections from owner gameplay source at 12.0–16.0s (village), 96.0–100.0s (open sky/cypresses), 268.5–272.5s (star rises). Hard cuts at 4.000 and 8.000. Within each section the visible movement is native game motion, not an edit. The recorded gameplay shows one world (Starry Town), not a transition to the documented second world.

`probe_extract.py --contact-interval 1 --scene-threshold .32 --skip-audio` yielded 12 reference frames and one contact sheet; candidate list **8.0s only**. The first hard cut to a similarly coloured view at 4.0s was missed; no extra scene candidate on this input. This is the upstream FFmpeg threshold behavior in one fixture, not a general accuracy benchmark. The contact sheet was visually inspected; operator corrected the list to the known authored three shots and kept native movement intact. Candidate time labels and representative frames are useful starting evidence, not an automatically correct narrative segmentation.

The manually authored `analysis.json` used actual reference images at 1/5/9s and three known source intervals. Upstream `build_workbooks.py --mode learning` wrote an actual Excel workbook, reopened in openpyxl: 4 sheets, 3 data rows, 3 embedded keyframes, expected image count met. SHA-256 `fd2a42c5abc91d65a0f21d5883a8966aecc6acf467541110abd51bb23c89c8da`. English content works but the upstream workbook headings remain Chinese; the fixed 220×102 image sizing can distort normal 16:9 frames. Table extraction and export, not human readability or semantically automatic creative analysis, verified. No replica workbook created. Subject/purpose and transferable principles are operator annotations, not model measurements. These would be reusable shot-review aids, not a mandate to use another spreadsheet for every film.
