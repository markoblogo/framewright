# A source-backed explainer in two formats

8 November 2026 · M3/M4, R04/R05/R09.
Worked cases: [Audio-Driven Avatars](../productions/audio-driven-avatars.md) and
[Junie Local](../productions/junie-local-hardware.md). Both films were accepted
with sound and their publication URLs supplied by the owner. This is bounded
production evidence, not measured efficiency, model qualification or audience success.

## Repeatable sequence

1. Freeze the viewer's question and primary-source claims. Separate vendor examples,
   original explanatory graphics and an actual local experiment. Date changing
   requirements; retain source/version snapshots. A code licence does not clear
   a demonstration's images or soundtrack.
2. Approve script and source/donor roles before assembly. Show a short moving pilot
   in both aspects. Junie's first diagram-only proposal needed a richer visual:
   an original hardware illustration led to a locally authored 3D scene, rather
   than an assumption that diagrams alone met the brief.
3. Reuse the accepted pilot opening, including its audio dependencies. Measure
   remaining English Supertonic-3 M1 phrases before setting scene boundaries.
   Use one timeline and audio mix for both outputs; keep a quiet original bed
   underneath speech instead of hard muting it.
4. Give each explanation visible action: source examples, moving data/layers,
   changing workload geometry or progressive emphasis. Compose large labels
   independently for portrait and landscape. A moving camera alone does not
   establish explanatory action; a few sampled stills do not establish motion quality.
5. Decode each encoded master, inspect layout and joins, measure sound, and test
   native forward/backward seeking before owner review. Use the existing
   [byte-range preview server](../../skills/video-production-router/references/preview.md).
   Technical checks, listening, creative acceptance and publication are separate.
6. Deliver separate platform copy pages, covers, comments/tags and direct master/SRT
   links. Inspect covers at full/reduced size and a simulated grid crop. Keep exact
   uploaded-cover identity and actual platform cropping unknown until observed.

For a later executed alternative to separate aspect layouts, see the
[centre-safe dual-format recipe](centre-safe-dual-format.md). Both techniques were
owner-accepted on two different90-second stories; measured export time does not
establish whole-workflow labour or monetary savings.

## Two useful variants

**Demonstration-led:** Avatars alternates labelled official examples with original
input/continuity diagrams. The same three-second excerpt is deliberately shown
without sound and then with source audio around 1:21/1:24. This two-pass exercise
is a local editorial example, not a lip-sync score or the dialogue of our narrator.
Exact donor-media public reuse remains unverified; keep that limitation with any reuse.

**Conceptual 3D:** Junie uses Blender 4.5.15 LTS Eevee for an unbranded laptop,
moving blocks, an orbiting camera and separating conceptual layers; Pillow/FFmpeg
compose the hardware cards and isometric resource animation. The Blender source
is 720p at 12 rendered frames per second; the pilot blends frames to 24fps.
Final 24fps files combine enlarged 720p material with native 1080p graphics and
separate aspect layouts. Upscaling does not recover native detail. This establishes
that selected scene/composition, not photorealistic animation or Junie inference.

## Correction and storage contract

Keep accepted masters, the final mix, speech stems, source claims, selected covers,
exact decisions and the selected assembler. Inspect its file reads before pruning.
Both full assemblers still read pilot MP4s/audio; Junie also reads 360 Blender JPEG
frames. They are current build dependencies, not disposable previews. Keep Avatar's
original demos for correction and unresolved source-use evidence.

Raw mixtures written anew by the assembler, unused phrase audio, backup scenes,
unreferenced inspection frames and rejected cover exports can retire after hash
and link checks. Preserve review-page poster files. Record removed names/hashes
and availability separately; historical QA receipts need not be rewritten as if
their former check images still existed. A future clip-cache redesign needs its own
bounded check and is not performed by this closeout.

## With Framewright / without it

**With Framewright:** retain the source snapshots, measured timeline, version-bound
owner decisions and publication URLs in the existing project journal. The optional
Radar adapter receives only YouTube publication links; acknowledgement is transport
evidence, not analytics, a preregistered experiment or an automatic recommendation.

**Without Framewright:** keep a source/rights sheet, timed script, WAV stems, scene
files, per-aspect composition, hash manifest and review decisions in an ordinary
folder. Assemble in an editor or the same local Blender/Pillow/FFmpeg tools, serve
the MP4 through a range-capable preview and prepare separate platform copy files.
This alternative is a written transferable process, not independent-user reproduction.

Next useful checks are a hash-bound derived-clip cache or improved subtitle alignment
on a fitting production. They remain proposed. These cases do not close REL-D/M6,
prove scheduled Radar feedback or justify a separate software release.
